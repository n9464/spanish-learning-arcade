This lesson shows where stress goes, when a written tilde appears, how to classify words, and how to justify every accent mark you write.

Step 1

Foundations: stress and written accent

What is a tilde?

In Spanish, a tilde is the written accent mark placed on a vowel: á, é, í, ó, ú. It tells you that the stressed syllable must be read in a specific place or that a special accent rule is active.

Stress is not the same as a tilde

Every Spanish word has a stressed syllable. That is where your voice naturally leans. Only some words also need a written tilde.

casa
ca sa

Stress exists, but there is no written tilde.

café
ca fé

The stress is on the last syllable, and a written tilde is required.

música
mú si ca

The stress is earlier, so the written tilde makes that visible.

Memory trick

Think of stress as the sound and the tilde as the spelling clue. Every word has the sound. Only some spellings need the clue.

Why “café” has a tilde

ca-fé is stressed on the last syllable. It is an aguda ending in a vowel, so it must carry a tilde.

Step 2

Agudas and graves / llanas

What makes a word aguda or grave?

These names do not mean “word with tilde” or “word without tilde.” They only describe where the stressed syllable is. First find the syllable you pronounce with the strongest voice. Then count from the end of the word.

How to classify the word

1. Split the word into syllables. 2. Find the stressed syllable. 3. Count backward from the end. If the stress is on the last syllable, it is aguda. If the stress is on the second-to-last syllable, it is grave / llana.

Palabras agudas

Stress on the last syllable
4 3 2 1

An aguda is a word whose stress falls on the last syllable: ca-fé, can-ción, re-loj, doc-tor.

The category comes from the sound pattern, not from the spelling. Some agudas have a tilde and some do not.

An aguda takes a tilde only if it ends in vowel, n, or s.

With tilde
  • ca-fé
  • can-ción
  • so-fá
  • com-pás
Without tilde
  • re-loj
  • ver-dad
  • fe-liz
  • doc-tor

So reloj is still aguda even though it has no tilde. The stress is final, so the word type is aguda; the ending j is simply the reason it is written without a tilde.

Palabras graves / llanas

Stress on the penultimate syllable
4 3 2 1

A grave or llana is a word whose stress falls on the penultimate syllable: ca-sa, jo-ven, ár-bol, a-zú-car.

Again, the name comes from the stress position. It does not automatically tell you whether there is a tilde.

A grave takes a tilde when it does not end in vowel, n, or s.

With tilde
  • lá-piz
  • ár-bol
  • fá-cil
  • a-zú-car
Without tilde
  • ca-sa
  • jo-ven
  • me-sa
  • re-su-men

This is why casa is grave even without a tilde: the stress is on ca, the second-to-last syllable. It has no tilde only because it ends in a vowel.

Side by side

canción aguda + ends in n = tilde
reloj aguda + ends in j = no tilde
árbol grave + ends in l = tilde
casa grave + ends in vowel = no tilde

Most important idea

Aguda and grave / llana are first of all stress categories. After you classify the word by sound, then you ask the second question: “Does this category, with this ending, require a tilde?”

Pattern summary

Agudas are marked when they finish with the “friendly” endings vowel, n, s. Graves are the opposite: they are marked when they finish with something else.

Step 3

Esdrújulas and sobresdrújulas

Palabras esdrújulas

Stress on the third syllable from the end
4 3 2 1

Esdrújulas always carry a tilde.

  • mú-si-ca
  • te-lé-fo-no
  • pá-ja-ro
  • sá-ba-do

Palabras sobresdrújulas

Stress before the third syllable from the end
4+ 3 2 1

Sobresdrújulas also always carry a tilde.

  • ex-plí-ca-me-lo
  • dí-ga-me-lo
  • cuén-ta-se-lo
  • re-pí-ta-se-lo

Important contrast

There are no correct esdrújulas or sobresdrújulas without a tilde. If you see telefono or explicamelo, the accent is missing.

Memory trick

The farther the stress moves to the left, the more likely the word needs a visible warning sign. Esdrújulas and sobresdrújulas always wear that sign.

Step 4

Hiatos, diphthongs, and triphthongs

Strong vowels a, e, o
Weak vowels i, u

Diphthong

Two vowels stay in the same syllable: ciu-dad, cau-sa.

If the word needs a tilde by the normal rule, the mark goes on the strong vowel or on the second weak vowel if both are weak.

Triphthong

Three vowels stay together in one syllable: es-tu-diáis.

The tilde goes on the strong middle vowel when a tilde is needed.

Hiato

Two vowels split into different syllables: pa-ís, ba-úl.

A stressed weak vowel í / ú takes a tilde to break the diphthong, even if the general aguda or grave rule would not force it.

Side by side

rio incorrect if you mean río
río rí-o, stressed weak vowel breaks the pair
pais incorrect if you mean país
país pa-ís, hiato with stressed í

Why “después” has a tilde

des-pués is an aguda ending in s. It needs a tilde, and the mark sits on the strong vowel inside the diphthong.

Step 5

Monosyllables and the diacritical tilde

Monosyllables

One-syllable words normally do not take a tilde: fue, dio, vio, fe, sol, ti, bien.

Diacritical tilde

Some short words take a tilde only to distinguish meaning: tú / tu, él / el, mí / mi, sí / si, sé / se, dé / de, té / te, más / mas, aún / aun.

Step 6

Interrogative and exclamative words

Question and exclamation forms

qué, cómo, cuándo, cuál, cuánto, dónde, quién take a tilde in direct questions and exclamations: ¿Qué dices? ¡Cómo llueve!

Indirect questions also keep the tilde

The accent stays even when there are no question marks: No sé dónde vive. Dime cuánto cuesta.

Meaning contrast

como I eat
cómo how
donde relative use in some contexts
dónde where, in a question or exclamation

Memory trick

If the word introduces a hidden question, keep the tilde: No sé dónde, Explícame cómo, Cuéntame quién.

Step 7

Workshop: pattern recognition and mixed drills

What strong writers do

They do not guess. They ask:

  1. Where is the stress?
  2. What word type is it?
  3. Does a special rule change the normal pattern?

Important edge case

Adverbs ending in -mente keep the accent of the base adjective: fácil → fácilmente, rápido → rápidamente.

Most common accent mistakes in Spanish

  • Forgetting that every word has stress, but not every word writes it.
  • Adding a tilde to every aguda or every grave instead of checking the ending.
  • Forgetting stressed í / ú in hiatos: río, país, oír.
  • Missing accents in indirect questions: No sé dónde está.
  • Confusing diacritical pairs such as tu / tú and se / sé.
  • Dropping the original accent in -mente adverbs: fácilmente.
