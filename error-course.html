Common cause

Most learner mistakes come from transfer: copying English logic, overusing one familiar form, or forgetting that Spanish marks gender, number, accent, or pronoun function more explicitly.

Step 1

How to think like an error corrector

Do not translate blindly

Correction starts by checking what the sentence is trying to say, then asking whether the Spanish form matches the tense, agreement, relationship, or accent pattern needed.

Correct the whole sentence

Even when one word is wrong, train yourself to rewrite the entire sentence correctly. That builds complete sentence control instead of isolated patching.

Verb

Wrong person, wrong tense, or wrong irregular form.

Agreement

Gender or number mismatch in nouns, articles, or adjectives.

Preposition

Using the English-style link instead of the Spanish one.

Accent

Missing tilde or confusion between meaning-changing forms.

Pronoun

Wrong pronoun function or wrong pronoun placement.

Step 2

Verb conjugation errors

Check the subject Who is doing the action: yo, tú, él, nosotros?
Check the time clue Words like ayer, mañana, siempre, ya often force a tense choice.
Check irregulars Common verbs like ir, ser, tener, hacer, and querer often break the pattern.

Step 3

Agreement errors

Number agreement

Plural subjects and plural nouns usually need plural articles, verbs, and adjectives.

Gender agreement

Article + noun + adjective often need matching masculine or feminine forms.

Watch out

Some nouns break the visual pattern: el problema, la mano.

Step 4

Wrong prepositions

Motion

a often points toward a destination: Voy a Madrid.

Location

en usually marks where something is: Estoy en Madrid.

Starting point

de often marks origin or where you come out from: Salgo de casa.

Step 5

Missing accents and meaning changes

tú / tu él / el qué / que está / esta papá / papa café / cafe frío / frio Perú / Peru

Step 6

Pronoun misuse and placement

Direct vs indirect

Lo / la receive the action. Le receives something.

Before a conjugated verb

Se lava. Pronouns usually go before a normal finite verb.

Attached to infinitives

Voy a levantarme. With infinitives, attachment is often possible or required.

Step 7

Mixed correction lab

Priority order

In mixed correction, first find the sentence part that breaks meaning most strongly: the verb, the relation marker, the agreement, the accent, or the pronoun chain.

One main target

These drills still center on one main correction family. That keeps the focus sharp and helps you build pattern memory instead of guessing.

Step 8

Final review and common traps

Most common learner causes

  • Copying English prepositions directly into Spanish
  • Using one familiar verb form for every subject
  • Ignoring gender and number because English marks them less
  • Skipping accents because typing feels slower
  • Mixing up lo / la / le or putting pronouns in the wrong place

Best correction habit

When you miss one, do not just memorize the corrected sentence. Ask: what clue should have warned me earlier? That is how correction turns into prevention.
