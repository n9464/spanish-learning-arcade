Goal

From infinitives to real control of Spanish verbs

This course is organized in the order that helps most learners: core present tense first, then major irregular patterns, then the practical tense system, then contrast practice and cumulative review.

Step 1

Foundations: how Spanish verbs are built

What is an infinitive?

The infinitive is the dictionary form of the verb: hablar, comer, vivir. It does not yet tell you who does the action or when it happens.

-ar, -er, -ir

Spanish verbs fall into three families based on their infinitive ending. The family tells you which ending pattern the verb will usually follow.

Stem + ending

habl + ar

Stem: the base meaning. Ending: the family marker or conjugated ending.

Conjugation

yo + habl o

Conjugating means changing the ending to match the person and the tense.

Regular vs irregular

Regular verbs keep the same stem and use normal endings. Irregular verbs break the pattern in the stem, the ending, or both.

yo

I

tú

you (informal)

él / ella / usted

he / she / you (formal)

nosotros / nosotras

we

vosotros / vosotras

you all (Spain)

ellos / ellas / ustedes

they / you all

Why pronouns often disappear

Spanish endings already show the subject: hablo clearly means I speak, so yo is often optional unless you want emphasis or contrast.

Quick logic

Read verbs in this order: tense or mood, then person, then check whether the verb is regular or irregular.

What stays the same

Usually the stem

The stem carries the core meaning: habl-, com-, viv-. In regular verbs, the stem stays stable.

What changes

The ending

The ending tells you who does the action. That is why hablo, hablas, hablamos all mean different subjects.

Negative forms and questions

No extra helper verb

No hablo mucho. ¿Vives aquí? Spanish usually keeps the same verb form and adds no or question intonation / punctuation.

Useful examples

Hablo con mi familia todos los días. I speak with my family every day.

No comemos carne hoy. We are not eating meat today.

¿Viven cerca de aquí? Do they live near here?

Memory trick

Present tense regular verbs are the base engine. If you can see the family quickly, the endings become much easier to attach.

Step 3

The most useful irregular present tense verbs

ser

soy, eres, es...
Completely irregular. Use for identity, origin, profession, time.

estar

estoy in yo, plus accent marks: estás, está. Use for location and states.

tener

tengo in yo and e → ie in most other forms: tienes, tiene.

hacer

hago in yo. The rest are mostly familiar: haces, hace.

ir

voy, vas, va... Completely irregular and essential for movement and near future.

venir

vengo in yo and e → ie: vienes, viene.

decir

digo in yo and e → i: dices, dice.

poder

o → ue: puedo, puedes. No stem change in nosotros / vosotros.

querer

e → ie: quiero, quieres. Very common with desires and requests.

ver

veo is the main irregular point. The rest are short and frequent.

dar

doy in yo. Short, high-frequency forms.

saber / conocer

sé and conozco are the key yo forms. The rest are much more regular.

High-value family trick

Many useful present irregulars belong to families: -go verbs (tengo, vengo, hago, digo), stem changers (puedo, quiero), and complete irregulars (soy, voy).

What to learn first

First memorize the yo form and the stem pattern. Then check whether nosotros returns to the base stem.

Step 4

Major verb patterns: stem changers, reflexives, gustar-type verbs, and more

Stem-changing families

e → ie: pensar, querer, empezar, sentir

o → ue: poder, volver, dormir

e → i: pedir, seguir

Usually no stem change in nosotros / vosotros in the present indicative.

Yo-form irregulars

salgo, pongo, traigo

conozco, parezco

hago, digo

Often the irregularity is concentrated in the yo form.

Spelling changes

llegar → llegué

empezar → empecé

seguir → sigo

These changes keep the sound consistent.

Reflexive verbs

me / te / se / nos / os / se+conjugated verb

Me levanto temprano. Nos acostamos tarde.

Gustar-type verbs

The thing liked controls the verb: Me gusta el libro. / Me gustan los libros.

Think: “The book is pleasing to me.”

Step 5

Infinitives, gerunds, participles, and the practical present systems

Infinitive

Base form

hablar, comer, vivir. Used after many verbs and prepositions: quiero comer, voy a estudiar.

Gerund

-ando / -iendo

hablando, comiendo, viviendo. Use with estar for the present progressive: estoy hablando.

Past participle

-ado / -ido

hablado, comido, vivido. Use with haber for the present perfect: he comido.

Present progressive

NOW

estar + gerund → action in progress right now.

Estoy leyendo. Estamos comiendo.

Near future

NOW → SOON

ir + a + infinitive → plan or near future: voy a salir.

Present perfect

PAST → NOW

haber + participle → completed action connected to the present: he visto.

Common irregular gerunds

decir → diciendo, dormir → durmiendo, pedir → pidiendo

Common irregular participles

hecho, dicho, visto, puesto, escrito, roto, vuelto, abierto, muerto

Step 6

Past time in action: preterite vs imperfect

Preterite

completed event

Use it for finished, bounded actions that move the story forward: Ayer comí.

Imperfect

background / habit / ongoing past

Use it for description, routine, age, time, and ongoing background: Cuando era niño...

Regular endings

Preterite and imperfect
Tense-ar-er / -ir
Preterite yo-é-í
Preterite tú-aste-iste
Preterite él / ella-ó-ió
Imperfect yo-aba-ía
Imperfect nosotros-ábamos-íamos
Imperfect ellos-aban-ían

Important irregulars

Preterite: fui, estuve, tuve, hice, pude, quise, vine, dije.

Imperfect: era, iba, veía.

Logic contrast

Preterite answers “what happened?”

Imperfect answers “what was going on?” or “what used to happen?”

Step 7

Future, conditional, and commands in practical use

Simple future

infinitive + ending

hablaré, comeré, viviré. Used for neutral future, prediction, or probability.

Conditional

infinitive + ía endings

hablaría, comería, viviría. Used for would, polite requests, and hypothetical results.

Commands

tell someone what to do

Habla. Coma. No salgas. Direct commands and polite commands use different forms.

Common irregular future / conditional stems

tendr-, har-, dir-, podr-, querr-, sabr-, vendr-, pondr-, saldr-, cabr-, habr-

Command contrast

Ven = direct tú command. Venga = polite usted command.

Near future vs simple future

Voy a llamar sounds planned and close.

Llamaré sounds more neutral, predictive, or formal.

Negative commands

Negative tú commands use the subjunctive pattern: No hables. No digas eso.

Step 8

Present subjunctive basics: when Spanish leaves the fact zone

When to use it

Use the present subjunctive after expressions of wish, emotion, doubt, influence, and importance: Quiero que vengas, Dudo que sea verdad.

How to form it

Start with the yo present form, drop the -o, and add the opposite endings: hable, coma, viva.

Present subjunctive endings

Essential irregulars

Indicative vs subjunctive

Sé que viene. = I know he is coming. Fact.

Quiero que venga. = I want him to come. Desired / not yet a fact.

Step 9

Contrast lab: the high-frequency choices that cause the most confusion

ser vs estar

ser: identity, classification, origin, time

estar: location, condition, temporary state, result

La sopa está fría hoy. / La sopa es una comida.

saber vs conocer

saber: facts, information, how to do something

conocer: to know people, places, familiarity

Sé la respuesta. / Conozco a Marta.

ir vs venir

ir goes away from the speaker’s location. venir comes toward the speaker or destination point.

Voy a tu casa. / ¿Vienes a mi casa?

por vs para with verbs

por: reason, exchange, movement through, “because of”

para: purpose, destination, deadline, recipient

Gracias por ayudarme. / Estudio para aprender.

Quick cheat sheet

Progressive: happening right now

Ir + a: near future / plan

Future: neutral future / prediction

Preterite: completed event

Imperfect: background / habit / description

Present perfect: completed action linked to now

Conditional: would / polite / hypothetical

Subjunctive: wish / doubt / emotion / influence

Commands: direct instruction

Most common Spanish verb mistakes

  • Using the infinitive instead of a conjugated form: yo comer instead of yo como.
  • Forgetting that stem changes usually disappear in nosotros / vosotros.
  • Confusing ser and estar when talking about temporary states.
  • Using gustar like English “I like” instead of matching the thing liked.
  • Mixing up preterite and imperfect in story narration.
  • Forgetting irregular future stems like tendr-, dir-, har-.
  • Using indicative after triggers that need the subjunctive.
