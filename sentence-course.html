Goal

From basic word order to advanced pronoun placement

This lesson shows the logic behind Spanish sentence order so you can stop translating English structure word by word.

Step 1

Basic word order: Spanish starts with SVO, but it is more flexible than English

Neutral Spanish order

María compra pan

Like English, the most neutral Spanish order is Subject + Verb + Object.

Spanish can move pieces for focus

El pan lo compra María

Spanish can front elements for emphasis or topic, especially if the meaning stays clear from context or pronouns.

English vs Spanish

EnglishThe teacher explains the lesson.
SpanishLa profesora explica la lección.

Start here when you are unsure. Learn the neutral order first, then learn the marked variations later.

Main idea

Spanish is not random. It is flexible. The default is simple, and the flexible order is usually used for emphasis, topic, rhythm, or to avoid repetition.

Step 2

Subject omission: why Spanish often leaves the subject pronoun out

With pronoun

Yo hablo español

More natural by default

Hablo español

The verb ending already tells you who the subject is.

Keep the subject when...

You want contrast, emphasis, or clarity: Yo no, pero ella sí.

Typical beginner mistake

Repeating yo, tú, él in every sentence because English does. Spanish usually sounds lighter without them.

Step 3

Adjective placement: after the noun is normal, but before the noun can change nuance or meaning

Default pattern

la casa roja

Most descriptive adjectives come after the noun.

Before the noun

gran hombre

Some adjectives move before the noun for style, emphasis, quantity, or a meaning shift.

Step 4

Negation: no, nunca, nadie, nada, tampoco, and how Spanish stacks negatives

Basic negation

No veo la película

No goes before the conjugated verb.

Negative chains

No veo nada nunca

Spanish can use multiple negative words together. This is normal, not “double negative” error logic from English.

Side by side

EnglishI do not see anything.
SpanishNo veo nada.
EnglishI do not go either.
SpanishYo tampoco voy.

Watch out

If a negative word like nunca or nadie comes before the verb, you usually do not add no: Nunca veo televisión.

Step 5

Question formation: no “do-support,” flexible inversion, and question words up front

Yes / no question

¿Comes pizza?

Spanish often keeps the normal verb form. Question meaning comes from punctuation, intonation, and context.

Question word question

¿Dónde vives?

Question words like qué, cuándo, dónde, cómo usually appear near the front.

No English-style helper verb

English asks Do you live here? Spanish says ¿Vives aquí? There is no extra helper like do.

Inversion is not required

¿Tú vienes? can appear for emphasis, but the more neutral version is often simply ¿Vienes?.

La veo

La replaces la película or another feminine singular direct object.

Indirect object

Le doy el libro

Le replaces the recipient: to her / to him / to you.

Step 7

Pronoun placement: before conjugated verbs, attached to infinitives and commands, plus clitic doubling

Before a conjugated verb

Lo quiero ver

Attached to infinitive / gerund / affirmative command

Quieroverlo
Estoymirándolo
Míralo

Clitic doubling

A María la veo. / Le di el libro a Ana.

Spanish often repeats the object with both a + noun and the matching pronoun. This is especially common with people and indirect objects.

Se + lo / la / los / las

When le / les appears before a direct-object pronoun, it changes to se: Se lo doy.

Step 8

Mastery lab: build, correct, and compare full Spanish sentences

Fast structure cheat sheet

Default order: Subject + Verb + Object

Omit subjects: when the verb already shows who

Adjectives: usually after the noun

Negation: no before the conjugated verb

Questions: no do-support; punctuation + intonation

Direct pronouns: lo, la, los, las

Indirect pronouns: le, les

Placement: before conjugated verbs, attached to infinitives / affirmative commands

Common learner mistakes

  • Keeping English word order too rigidly and assuming Spanish can never move pieces.
  • Repeating subject pronouns in every sentence.
  • Putting descriptive adjectives before the noun by default.
  • Using English-style negation logic and avoiding normal Spanish negative chains.
  • Building questions with an English helper verb pattern.
  • Mixing up direct and indirect object pronouns.
  • Putting pronouns after a conjugated verb when they should go before it.
  • Missing clitic doubling in sentences like A María la veo.
