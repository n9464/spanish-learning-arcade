Step 1

Pronoun map: subject pronouns and the basic idea of replacement

What pronouns do

Yo hablo español
drop the subject if the verb already shows it
Hablo español

Pronouns often replace or point back to information so the sentence does not keep repeating full nouns.

Subject pronoun set

yo tú él / ella / usted nosotros vosotros ellos / ustedes

Subject pronouns tell you who does the action, but Spanish often omits them because the verb ending already carries that meaning.

First decision

Ask whether the pronoun is the doer, the thing affected, the person receiving, or part of a fixed relationship like a relative clause.

Avoid redundancy

Spanish rarely needs repeated subject pronouns like yo yo or yo estudio y yo trabajo unless there is contrast.

Step 2

Direct object pronouns: replace the thing directly affected

Direct object map

Ask what? or whom?
PronounReplacesExample
lomasculine singular thing or personVeo el libro. → Lo veo.
lafeminine singular thing or personVeo la película. → La veo.
losmasculine pluralCompro los zapatos. → Los compro.
lasfeminine pluralLeo las cartas. → Las leo.

Step-by-step substitution

Veo la película
replace the direct object noun
La veo

Redundancy warning

Do not keep the full noun unless you have a special topicalized structure. La veo la película is not the normal replacement pattern.

Step 3

Indirect object pronouns: replace the recipient or beneficiary

Indirect object map

Ask to whom? or for whom?
PronounMeaningExample
meto meMe dan ayuda.
teto youTe escribo.
leto him / her / ustedLe doy el libro.
nosto usNos mandan un mensaje.
osto you all (Spain)Os cuento la historia.
lesto them / ustedesLes preparo la cena.

Step-by-step substitution

Doy el libro a Ana
replace the recipient
Le doy el libro

Do not confuse the roles

The thing given stays the direct object. The person receiving it becomes the indirect object.

Step 4

Placement rules: use the verb form to decide where the pronoun goes

Conjugated verb?

Put the pronoun before it: lo veo, me levanto.

Infinitive or gerund?

Either before the first verb or attached: lo quiero ver / quiero verlo.

Affirmative command?

Attach the pronoun: dímelo, siéntate.

Negative command?

Put the pronoun before the verb: no me lo digas.

Transformation chain

ConjugatedLo quiero.
Infinitive option 1Lo quiero ver.
Infinitive option 2Quiero verlo.
Negative commandNo lo veas.

Common wrong forms

Avoid quiero lo ver, doy le, and no dímelo. The verb form controls placement.

Step 6

Relative pronouns: connect a noun to extra information without repeating it

Relative pronoun system

Linking words
PronounMain useExample
quegeneral connectorLa casa que veo
quien / quienespeople, often after prepositionsLa mujer con quien hablo
el cual / la cual...more formal, often after prepositionsLa ciudad en la cual vivo
cuyo / cuya...whoseEl estudiante cuyo libro encontré

Visual link

la mujer con quien hablo

The relative pronoun points back to la mujer and starts the extra clause.

Why this matters

Relative pronouns stop repetition. Instead of two separate noun phrases, the pronoun links the second clause back to the first noun.

Step 7

Demonstrative pronouns: point to this, that, and that over there

Distance map

este / esta / esto ese / esa / eso aquel / aquella / aquello

Use them to point near the speaker, near the listener, or farther away.

Noun vs pronoun

este libro
remove the noun
este

As pronouns, these forms stand alone. Modern standard Spanish does not require accent marks on them.

Step 8

Double object pronouns: combine indirect and direct objects cleanly

Transformation chain

Doy el libro a Ana
replace recipient
Le doy el libro
replace the direct object too
Se lo doy

Main warning

Le / les changes to se before lo / la / los / las. Do not say le lo doy.

Common pronoun mistakes

  • Repeating subject pronouns when the verb already shows the subject.
  • Keeping the noun after replacing it with a direct object pronoun.
  • Mixing up direct and indirect objects in give / send / tell sentences.
  • Putting pronouns after a conjugated verb in ordinary statements.
  • Writing le lo instead of se lo.
  • Using the wrong relative pronoun after a preposition.
