Step 1

Foundations: prepositions connect ideas by showing relationships

Think in relationships

verb
destination
location
person
reason / purpose

Prepositions tell you how the rest of the sentence connects to the action: where, toward whom, from where, why, with what, or without what.

Stop translating word by word

EnglishI am at home.
SpanishEstoy en casa.
EnglishI am going home.
SpanishVoy a casa.

The right Spanish preposition depends on the relationship in the sentence, not on a one-to-one English match.

Main rule

Ask a relationship question first: to where?, in where?, for what purpose?, because of what?, with whom?.

Why learners struggle

English often uses the same preposition in places where Spanish splits the logic into different choices, especially with por / para and a / en.

Step 3

Motion vs location: use a to go, use en to be

Motion toward a place

Voy a la tienda

Use a when the action moves toward a destination.

Location in a place

Estoy en la tienda

Use en when the sentence describes being in or at a place.

A vs en

DestinationVamos a Madrid.
LocationEstamos en Madrid.
DestinationLlego a casa.
LocationTrabajo en casa.

Fast test

If you can ask to where?, you probably need a. If you can ask where?, you probably need en.

Step 4

The personal a: use a before a specific human direct object

With a person

Veo a María

When the direct object is a specific person, Spanish usually adds the personal a.

With a thing

Veo la película

No personal a is used before ordinary things.

Think animate and specific

The personal a is strongest with known people, names, and specific pets treated like people.

Contrast

Busco a mi profesor. but Busco mi cuaderno.

Step 5

Por vs para: cause, route, exchange, purpose, destination, and deadlines

Deep contrast table

One of the most important distinctions in Spanish
Useporpara
Cause / reasonLo hice por ti.Not used for cause
Route / throughCaminamos por el parque.Not used for route
Exchange / priceLo compré por diez euros.Not used for price
DurationEstudié por dos horas.Not used for duration
Purpose / goalNot the main useEstudio para aprender.
RecipientNot the main useEste regalo es para Ana.
DestinationNot the main useSalgo para Madrid.
DeadlineNot the main useLa tarea es para mañana.

Meaning shortcut

por often looks backward: cause, means, route, exchange.

para often looks forward: goal, recipient, destination, deadline.

Ask the right question

Because of what? through where? in exchange for what? points to por. For what purpose? for whom? by when? points to para.

Common verb-preposition pairings: learn them as fixed meaning patterns

  • depender de
  • hablar de
  • acordarse de
  • venir de

en

  • pensar en
  • entrar en
  • estar en
  • trabajar en

con / por / para / sobre

  • soñar con
  • preocuparse por
  • prepararse para
  • hablar sobre

Learn the pairing as one idea

Instead of memorizing pensar and later adding en, learn pensar en as a single meaning unit: to think about.

Real-life examples

Pienso en mi familia. / Depende de ti. / Soñé con el examen.

Common learner mistakes

  • Using en after movement verbs like ir when the sentence needs a destination.
  • Forgetting the personal a before a specific person.
  • Using para for duration instead of por.
  • Using por for a recipient instead of para.
  • Translating English prepositions word for word in verb pairings.
